This pillar has stood in the open air for about 1,600 years. It was already over a thousand years old when the Taj Mahal was built.
So who made it?

चन्द्र
A king called Chandra.
That's the name in its Sanskrit inscription, carved in Brahmi script. Most historians identify him as Chandragupta II of the Gupta empire, around 400 CE. The inscription has no date, so it's still debated.
And it was never meant to stand where it stands today…
04 / 08
It was moved.
The inscription says it was raised as a standard of Vishnu on a hill called Vishnupada. Delhi has no such hill.
Many scholars place the hill at Udayagiri in Madhya Pradesh; others disagree. Who carried six tonnes of iron to Delhi, and when, is argued too.

06 / 08
Phosphorus.
Modern furnaces add lime, which pulls phosphorus out into the slag. The smiths who made this iron used no lime, so far more phosphorus stayed in the metal.
Diagram · dots = phosphorus
Normally that's a flaw. Here, it became armour…
07 / 08
It grew its own rust shield.
Helped by the phosphorus, a thin protective layer formed on the surface within about three years and then grew very slowly, keeping the iron beneath from rusting away.
Diagram · layer not to scale
After 1,600 years: about 1/20 of a millimetre thick.
08 / 08
For centuries, visitors hugged it backwards.
Back to the pillar, arms behind, trying to make your hands meet: it was said to bring luck. All that touching wore the lower part smooth.
