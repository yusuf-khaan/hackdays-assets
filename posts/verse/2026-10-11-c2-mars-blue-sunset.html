First, Earth
Why our sky is blue and our sunsets red.
Diagram · not to scale
Air molecules scatter blue light across the whole sky. At sunset, sunlight crosses far more air, most of the blue is scattered out of the beam, and reds and oranges reach you.
Now go to Mars. It barely has air…
04 / 07
<1%
Mars's atmosphere is less than 1% as dense as Earth's: too thin for air to turn the sky blue.
So fine dust colours it instead. By day, the Martian sky looks butterscotch, a yellowish brown.

The dust lets blue through, straight ahead.

NASA's explanation: the grains are the right size to pass blue light forward, close to the Sun's direction. Scientists are still refining the exact physics.
